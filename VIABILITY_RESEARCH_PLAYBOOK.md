# Viability Research Playbook
### AI Data Analyst for Creators & Small Businesses — and 2 backup ideas

> This file extends your *AI Product Weekend Research Booklet*. The booklet tells you **how to research**. This playbook tells you **how to decide whether the idea is actually viable** — with falsifiable tests, kill-criteria, and a scoring model that prevents you from fooling yourself.

**Author context:** Harveer Mor — data analytics interest, NaagaAstro content channel (your built-in test lab), Haryana/Haryanvi local advantage.

**The one rule of this document:** You are not trying to *prove the idea works*. You are trying to *find the fastest, cheapest evidence that it does NOT work*. If it survives every attempt to kill it, then it's viable.

---

## 0. The Core Viability Question (read this first)

A business idea is viable only if **all five** of these are true at once. Miss one and it collapses:

| # | Condition | The question that tests it | How you'll know it's FALSE (kill signal) |
|---|-----------|----------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| 1 | **Real pain** | Do people already lose time/money/sleep over this? | They shrug. They can't name the last time it hurt. |
| 2 | **Reachable users** | Can YOU get in front of 100 of them cheaply? | You'd need ads/agencies/cold outreach you can't afford. |
| 3 | **They already pay** | Is money already moving for this problem? | "I'd use it if free." No existing spend anywhere. |
| 4 | **Small MVP works** | Can a manual/no-code version deliver value this month? | Needs full app + APIs + approvals before *any* value. |
| 5 | **You have an edge** | Why you, vs. the 50 existing tools? | No distribution, no niche, no unfair advantage. |

> Your booklet already nails #1, #2, #4. This playbook adds rigor to **#3 (willingness to pay)** and **#5 (your edge)** — the two that kill most first-time founders.

## 1. The Brutal Truth About This Specific Idea (so you research with eyes open)

Before you spend a weekend, know the landscape you're walking into. This is **not discouragement** — it's the map of where the landmines are.

### What's genuinely strong about the creator-analytics idea
- **You are your own first customer** (NaagaAstro). You can build, test, and demo on real data on day one. Almost no founder gets this.
- The pain is **real and emotional**: "Why did my reel flop?" is asked millions of times a day.
- The **wedge is sharp**: not "another dashboard" but *diagnosis + next actions*. That's a positioning gap competitors genuinely leave open for small/Indian/Hinglish creators.
- **MVP can be 100% manual** (screenshots in → PDF report out). Zero code needed to test demand.

### Where the landmines are (research THESE, not just the market size)
| Landmine | Why it matters | What to verify this weekend |
|----------|----------------|------------------------------|
| **"Nice-to-have" tax** | Analytics advice is often valued at ₹0 because creators think they *should* know it themselves. | Find anyone who *already pays* for an audit/course/agency. If nobody pays → weak. |
| **Free alternatives are everywhere** | Instagram's own Insights, free YouTube gurus, ChatGPT screenshots. | Ask interviewees: "Why wouldn't you just paste this into ChatGPT yourself?" Their answer IS your moat (or its absence). |
| **Churn is brutal for creator tools** | Creators sign up, get one report, cancel. | Test: would they want it *weekly*? Repeat-usage = survival. One-time = dead. |
| **Competitor data is legally/technically hard** | You cannot reliably scrape competitors at scale (Instagram ToS + blocking). | Decide early: lead with *their own data + benchmarks*, treat competitor-compare as a later "wow" feature, not the core. |
| **Indian price ceiling is low** | ₹299–999/mo is the realistic band. Margins are thin per user. | Validate the *band*, then math out: how many users to make this worth your time? |

### Honest verdict before research
This is a **viable wedge with a real risk of being a "vitamin not a painkiller."** The entire weekend should hunt for the **painkiller signal**: someone who is *already spending money* and is *frustrated*. Find 3 of those = green light. Find zero = pivot.

## 2. Falsifiable Hypotheses (turn vibes into pass/fail tests)

Write these down. Each has a **kill threshold**. If reality comes in below the threshold, that part of the idea is *dead* and you stop arguing with yourself.

| # | Hypothesis | Test method | PASS threshold | KILL threshold |
|---|-----------|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| H1 | Creators feel real pain about "what to post next / why did this flop" | 5–10 interviews + 20 scraped complaints | ≥6/10 describe it unprompted with emotion | ≤2/10 care |
| H2 | They don't already solve this well enough for free | Interview Q: "what do you do today?" | ≥5/10 say "guess / nothing / confused" | Most say "ChatGPT is fine" |
| H3 | Money already moves for this | Q: "what have you paid for?" | ≥3/10 have paid for course/agency/audit/tool | 0/10 ever paid |
| H4 | A manual PDF report delivers real "aha" | Deliver 10 free manual reports | ≥3 say "this was genuinely useful, what was missing?" | ≤1 engages after delivery |
| H5 | They'd pay ₹299–999/mo for it recurring | Q after value shown | ≥3/10 give a concrete yes + price | 0 concrete yes |
| H6 | You can reach 100 of them for ~₹0 | Your own audience + DMs + 2 communities | You list a credible path to 100 | You'd need paid ads to find anyone |

**Decision rule:** Need **H1, H4, and (H3 OR H5)** to all PASS to proceed. H3/H5 is the money test — at least one must hold.

## 3. The "Already Paying" Hunt (the single most important research task)

90% of failed founders skipped this. Spend disproportionate energy here.

You are looking for **proof that money already moves** for this problem. Search and screenshot:

1. **Course/audit sellers** — Search Instagram/YouTube for "Instagram growth audit ₹", "reel strategy course India". If people *sell* audits, people *buy* audits → demand confirmed.
2. **Agency pricing** — DM 2–3 small social media agencies as a prospective client. Note their monthly fee. That's the pain priced by the market.
3. **Existing tool reviews** — On app stores / G2 / Reddit, find people *paying for and complaining about* Metricool, Later, Vista Social, Sprout. Complaints from *paying* users = gold (proven payer + unmet need).
4. **Fiverr/Upwork** — Search "Instagram analytics report" / "social media audit". Active gigs with reviews = recurring paid demand you could productize.
5. **"Done-for-you" Telegram/WhatsApp groups** — many Indian creators pay coaches monthly.

> **Output:** A one-page list titled *"Evidence money already moves."* If this page is empty after a weekend → that is your answer. The idea is a vitamin. Pivot or re-scope.

## 4. Reverse Competitor Analysis (find the hate, not the absence)

Your booklet says "find what users still hate." Here's the structured way:

For each of **6–8 tools** (Metricool, Later, Vista Social, Sprout Social, Hootsuite, Iconosquare, Not Just Analytics, Buffer), fill this:

| Field | What to capture |
|-------|-----------------|
| Who it's for | Enterprise? Agency? Solo creator? |
| Price (₹/mo equiv.) | The real number |
| 1-star / 2-star themes | Copy exact complaint sentences |
| "I wish it..." quotes | The feature gap in their words |
| Why a small Indian creator wouldn't use it | Price? Complexity? No Hinglish? No advice? |

**What you're hunting for — the recurring complaint pattern:**
- "Shows numbers but doesn't tell me what to *do*" → confirms your wedge.
- "Too expensive for what I need" → confirms price gap.
- "Built for agencies, overwhelming for me" → confirms small-creator gap.

> 3+ tools with the *same* "doesn't tell me what to do" complaint = **strong validation of your positioning.** If instead reviews say "great advice, very actionable" → the gap is already filled; rethink your wedge.

## 5. The 30-Day "Concierge MVP" Experiment (do the work by hand first)

This is the real test. **Build nothing.** Be the AI yourself (with ChatGPT/Claude as your tool).

### Week 1 — Recruit
- Goal: 10 small creators/business pages (start with your NaagaAstro network + DMs).
- Ask only for: insights screenshots/CSV, their niche, their goal.
- Track recruiting conversion: *how many DMs to get 10 yeses?* This number IS your future customer-acquisition difficulty.

### Week 2 — Deliver manually
For each person, produce a 1-page report:
- ✅ What worked (top posts + *why* — hook, topic, format, timing)
- ❌ What failed (and the likely reason)
- 📈 3 patterns in their data
- 🎯 Next 10 post ideas with hooks
- ⏰ Best posting time + caption/SEO notes

Use Claude/ChatGPT to draft, *you* add judgment. Deliver as PDF/Google Doc. **Time yourself per report** — that's your future unit cost.

### Week 3 — Extract truth
Ask every recipient three questions only:
1. "What part was most useful?" (tells you the real product)
2. "What was missing / wrong?" (tells you the gap)
3. "Would you pay ₹299 / ₹499 / ₹999 a month for this weekly?" (the money test)

### Week 4 — Score against kill-criteria
| Metric | Green (build MVP) | Yellow (iterate) | Red (pivot) |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|-------------|
| Concrete "yes I'd pay" | ≥3 of 10 | 1–2 | 0 |
| "Most useful part" consistent | Clear pattern | Mixed | Random/none |
| Asked for next report unprompted | ≥3 | 1–2 | 0 |
| Time per report | <45 min | 45–90 min | >90 min (won't scale) |

> **Decision:** All-green or mostly-green → build the web MVP (upload data → weekly action plan). Red on the money row → the idea failed its most important test. That's a *successful* weekend: you saved months.

## 6. Upgraded Scoring Model (weighted — not all criteria are equal)

Your booklet's 1–5 table is good but treats every factor equally. They're not equal. Use weights:

| Criterion | Weight | Creator/Analyst | MSME | Agriculture |
|-----------|:------:|:---------------:|:----:|:-----------:|
| Pain intensity | ×3 | __/5 | __/5 | __/5 |
| Willingness to pay (proven) | ×3 | __/5 | __/5 | __/5 |
| Users easy for YOU to reach | ×3 | __/5 | __/5 | __/5 |
| Repeat usage / retention | ×2 | __/5 | __/5 | __/5 |
| Can build small MVP fast | ×2 | __/5 | __/5 | __/5 |
| Your founder advantage | ×2 | __/5 | __/5 | __/5 |
| Competition weakness/gap | ×1 | __/5 | __/5 | __/5 |
| Low regulation/risk | ×1 | __/5 | __/5 | __/5 |
| Personal interest | ×1 | __/5 | __/5 | __/5 |
| Time to first revenue | ×1 | __/5 | __/5 | __/5 |
| **WEIGHTED TOTAL** | | **/95** | **/95** | **/95** |

> Multiply each score by its weight, sum the column. **Highest weighted total wins** — and notice the ×3 rows (pain, payment, reach) dominate, exactly as they should. An idea that scores 5 on "personal interest" but 1 on "willingness to pay" should lose. The math enforces honesty.

## 7. Idea-Specific Viability Notes

### Track 1 — AI Creator/Data Analyst (your #1)
- **Strongest because:** you're your own lab, MVP is manual, wedge is sharp.
- **Biggest risk:** "vitamin not painkiller" + free ChatGPT substitution.
- **Make-or-break test:** Section 3 (already-paying hunt) + H5 (recurring pay).
- **If it passes:** narrow hard — e.g. *"AI growth analyst for Indian Reels creators under 50k followers."* Niche beats broad.

### Track 2 — MSME Tools (backup)
- **Stronger pain, weaker distribution.** A shopkeeper feels WhatsApp-lead-loss daily, but you can't reach 100 shopkeepers from your couch.
- **Viability hinges on a narrow wedge:** e.g. *"WhatsApp follow-up assistant for coaching centres"* — NOT "AI for MSMEs."
- **Key test:** Can you reach them? (Walk into 5 local businesses this weekend. If outreach is painful at n=5, it's fatal at n=100.)

### Track 3 — Agriculture/Haryana (long-shot, real edge)
- **Your language advantage is genuine**, but **trust + liability risk is high** (wrong crop advice costs real money).
- **Only-safe MVP:** an *intake/triage assistant* that organizes symptoms and routes to a human expert — never a final "AI doctor."
- **Key test:** Who do farmers trust *first*? If the answer is never "an app," the direct-to-farmer model is dead; the viable version is a **tool you sell to dealers/co-ops**, not farmers.

## 8. Anti-Self-Deception Checklist (the mistakes that kill weekend research)

- [ ] I searched for **proof of payment**, not just market size.
- [ ] I asked "what do you do *today*?" — not "would you use this?"
- [ ] I wrote pain in **users' exact words**, not my paraphrase.
- [ ] I talked to **strangers**, not just supportive friends.
- [ ] I looked for the **painkiller** (already paying + frustrated), not just polite interest.
- [ ] I let myself **kill an idea** that failed its threshold instead of rationalizing.
- [ ] I tested **repeat-usage**, not one-time curiosity.
- [ ] I did NOT touch logo, name, branding, or code.

## 10. Monday Actions (only if green)

1. Pick the single narrowest niche that passed (e.g. "Indian Reels creators under 50k").
2. Write the *one-sentence promise*: "Send me your last 30 days of insights, get a weekly plan of what to post next."
3. Line up the next 10 free/paid concierge reports — keep doing it by hand until the queue hurts. *Then* build the MVP to relieve the pain. Not before.
